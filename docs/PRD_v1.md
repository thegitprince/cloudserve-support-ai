## 2. Executive Summary & Problem Framing

CloudServe asked for a chatbot. The Project Brief is explicit that building one would satisfy the request and fail the assignment — a chatbot is a delivery mechanism, not an answer to why tickets take 8–12 hours for a first reply, why first-contact resolution (FCR) sits at 42–43.8%, or why escalations cost roughly 24x a resolved ticket's handling time (`stage_1_discovery_findings.md`, Section 2).

**Problem statement (carried across unchanged from the Stage 1 workbook, Section 6):**

> CloudServe's support team is not simply short-staffed or slow to reply — most of the tickets it handles already have a correct answer sitting in its own documentation, but agents can't reliably find that answer under time pressure, so they either reconstruct it from memory, pull it from an unreviewed personal file, or forward it to a more senior engineer who has to start over without knowing what was already tried. That pattern eats time on tickets that were never actually hard, drives up the first-contact resolution shortfall leadership is judged on, and — contrary to the assumption that CloudServe's best customers are already well served — is currently hitting its premium enterprise customers hardest of all. Fixing this means making the right existing answer easy to find and trust for the tickets that have one, and handing everything else to a person with a clear note on what's already known, while never letting the system touch the handful of ticket types — security, compliance, billing commitments, and anything with no answer on file — where a wrong guess does real damage.

This PRD specifies a system that: (1) correctly identifies which tickets are answerable from CloudServe's existing 29-article knowledge base (71.4% of volume, per discovery Section 2), (2) retrieves and cites the correct article despite customers not using the article's own wording (the findability gap Sofia and Ines both independently describe), (3) drafts grounded, cited responses only for tickets it can defend, and (4) escalates everything else — including the 17.4% of tickets flagged `must_not_auto_respond` — with a triage packet rather than a bare forward, directly answering Daniel's complaint that escalations currently arrive with no summary.

## 4. Functional Requirements

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Discovery Evidence | Acceptance Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FR-01** | The system shall ingest tickets from all four channels (email, chat, docs_comment, forum) and normalize them into one internal representation (channel, subject, body, customer_tier, customer_region, language_fluency, received_at) before any downstream processing. | Must | Project Brief §01, "The four channels," and §04 Component table ("Ingest... Channel-specific quirks leaking into every downstream component" is the named failure mode); dataset schema confirms channel is a first-class field (`development_tickets.json` schema, 42.4%/31.0%/15.6%/11.0% split, Stage 1 Section 2) | Given a ticket from any of the four channel values, the normalized record contains identical required fields regardless of source channel; a ticket with an empty `subject` (as chat tickets are documented to have) does not error or drop required fields. |
| **FR-02** | The system shall predict an `intent` (one of the 22 classes present in the dataset) and a calibrated confidence score for each normalized ticket. | Must | Project Brief §04 Component table, Classify: "produce a calibrated confidence score... A confidence number that does not actually correspond to the probability of being right" is the named failure mode; Stage 1 Section 3 confirms 22 intent classes with uneven distribution (13–29 tickets/class) | For a held-out sample, reported confidence is within 5 points of observed accuracy on that sample (NFR-03); every classified ticket carries both a predicted intent and a confidence value, never one without the other. |
| **FR-03** | The system shall predict an `urgency` level (high/medium/low) for each ticket, independent of intent, to support prioritization within the queue. | Must | Dataset schema `labels.urgency`; Project Brief §01 notes chat customers "abandon the conversation if they do not get [an answer] fast," i.e., urgency is channel- and content-dependent, not solely a function of intent | Urgency is predicted for 100% of ingested tickets; tickets flagged high-urgency are routed to a resolution path (automated or escalated) before equal-confidence medium/low-urgency tickets in the same batch. |
| **FR-04** | The system shall classify every ticket into exactly one of two routing categories — **mandatory escalation** or **discretionary** (routable to either auto-respond or escalate based on confidence) — before any auto-response is considered, using a deterministic, code-level `MANDATORY_ESCALATION_INTENTS` lookup table as the authoritative decision (the LLM's own `is_mandatory_escalation` flag from FR-02's triage step is a secondary, monitored signal, never the sole gate). The mandatory set is `{compliance_request, security_incident, feature_request, unclear_request, data_residency}`. | Must | Stage 1 Section 6, Contradiction 1: of 189 escalated dev-set tickets, 87 (46.0%) are policy-mandatory and 102 (54.0%) are discretionary — matching Daniel's "about half" claim. Full enumeration of `development_tickets.json` (not a sample) confirms `compliance_request` (26/26), `security_incident` (26/26), `feature_request` (20/20), and `unclear_request` (15/15) are each 100% flagged `must_not_auto_respond`, summing to exactly 87; no other intent is flagged at the full-intent level. `data_residency` is flagged at only 69% of its own tickets (20/29) in the data, but is added to the mandatory set as a deliberate policy extension beyond that data point, directly on Daniel Okonkwo's (Interview 3) testimony: "anything involving data location... we get those wrong occasionally even as humans and the consequence is a compliance problem." `billing_query` is deliberately NOT added to this set — Daniel's separate concern about billing commitments is addressed instead at the FR-07 guardrail layer, since most billing_query tickets (54.2% non-escalated historically) are legitimately answerable and a blanket escalation would over-correct against that evidence. | Every ticket in the 5-intent mandatory set is escalated regardless of retrieval or generation confidence — this check happens before generation, not after, so no draft is ever produced for a mandatory-escalation ticket; tested with the 87 dataset-confirmed tickets as a regression suite, plus a dedicated test asserting all `data_residency` tickets route to mandatory escalation via the code-level table regardless of their individual `must_not_auto_respond` value. |
| **FR-05** | For discretionary tickets, the system shall perform semantic retrieval over the 29-article knowledge base using Chroma with `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings, returning the top-k passages most relevant to the ticket's body text (not just its title-keyword overlap). | Must | Project Brief §09 Technical constraints (Chroma, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 specified stack); Stage 1 Section 6, Contradiction 3: documentation is accurate but unused because "our internal search matches on titles and exact terms, and customers do not describe problems in the words I used for the title" (Ines, Interview 4); 71.4% of tickets are `answerable_from_docs=True` | For tickets where `expected_doc_ids` is populated in the dataset, the correct `doc_id` appears in the top-k retrieved set at a rate meeting the citation-accuracy NFR (NFR-03); retrieval runs against ticket body semantics, verified by a test case using Sofia's named example wording mismatch ("my deployment keeps dying" vs. an article titled "resolving container health check failures"). |
| **FR-06** | For discretionary tickets with retrieval confidence above the routing threshold, the system shall generate a drafted response grounded only in the retrieved passages, and every generated response shall cite the specific `doc_id`(s) it drew from. | Must | Project Brief §04 Component table, Generate: "Draft an answer grounded in the retrieved passages, with citations... Fluent text that is not actually supported by the sources cited" is the named failure mode; Interview 4 (Ines): "I would want to know which article an answer came from, so that when an answer is wrong I can tell whether the article is wrong or the system misread it" | No generated response is released without at least one `doc_id` citation; a response with zero supporting retrieved passages above threshold is never generated — the ticket routes to escalation instead (see FR-04/FR-07 guardrail). |
| **FR-07** | The system shall validate every drafted response against the ticket's `must_not_claim` list (where available) and a general forbidden-claims/PII check before release, and shall block release — routing to escalation instead — if any forbidden claim or PII is detected. | Must | Dataset Guide, ground-truth schema: "An answer that makes any claim in [`must_not_claim`] is a governance problem rather than a quality one"; Project Brief §07 Governance conditions: "Private data in outbound responses: Zero occurrences. There is no acceptable rate"; Marcus (Interview 1): "I would rather it said nothing than said something wrong" | Zero `must_not_claim` violations and zero PII occurrences across the full validation set (NFR-04); a response that fails this check is logged as a blocked release with the specific rule triggered, and the ticket is escalated, not silently dropped. |
| **FR-08** | For every escalated ticket (mandatory or discretionary), the system shall generate a Triage Escalation Packet containing: the original ticket, the predicted intent/urgency and confidence, the documentation passages retrieved (if any), and an explicit statement of what the system was not confident about or why it escalated. | Must | Interview 3 (Daniel), verbatim: "If an escalation arrived saying here is the ticket, here is what I think it is about, here is the documentation that seemed relevant, and here is specifically what I was not confident about, I would be twice as fast... I don't need it to be right. I need it to show its working." This is the single most specific, directly quotable requirement in the discovery evidence. | Every escalated ticket (100%) is accompanied by a packet with all four named fields populated (ticket, intent/urgency+confidence, retrieved docs or explicit "none retrieved," and a stated reason for escalation); a packet is never just the forwarded raw ticket. |
| **FR-09** | The system shall write a complete record to the Decision Log for every ticket processed, matching the schema in the Governance Framework (input, prediction, confidence, sources used, action taken, reason for the action). | Must | CLAUDE.md §3 Traceability & Governance: "Every ticket processed must write a complete record to the Decision Log DB matching the exact schema in Governance_Framework"; Project Brief §04: "the decision log sits in the persistence layer... Every decision your system takes has to be reconstructable months later"; §08: "A decision log that captures, for every automated decision, the input, the prediction, the confidence, the sources used, the action taken and the reason for it" | 100% of processed tickets (development, validation, and hidden sets) produce one matching Decision Log record each; a record can be used, unaided, to reconstruct why any single ticket was auto-answered or escalated, satisfying Marcus's stated compliance-review need (Interview 1). |
| **FR-10** | The system shall provide an administrative kill switch that immediately halts all automated response generation (routing every ticket to escalation) without requiring a deployment or code change, and that is itself a logged, auditable action. | Must | Project Brief §08: "A stated position on what your system must never do, and the mechanism that enforces it" and the risk matrix requirement that the darkest-risk items have a written mitigation before build; CLAUDE.md §2, Unattended Gate Rule — the harness must degrade safely rather than failing open; Marcus (Interview 1): "If it sends something wrong to a customer... I would rather it said nothing than said something wrong" names the exact failure mode a kill switch exists to stop mid-incident | Triggering the kill switch causes 100% of subsequently ingested tickets to route to escalation within one processing cycle, with no change to retrieval or classification components required; the toggle action itself is written to the Decision Log with timestamp and operator identity; re-enabling is a separate, equally logged action. |
| **FR-11** | The system shall compute and report response-quality and routing-outcome metrics segmented by `customer_tier`, `customer_region`, and `language_fluency`, as a standing fairness check rather than a one-off audit. | Should | Project Brief §08: "A fairness audit comparing the quality of responses across customer groups, with the method and the sample size stated"; Stage 1 Section 6, Contradiction 2 — enterprise tier currently has the worst FCR (37.3%), slowest resolution (483.7 min), and highest escalation rate (62.7%) of the three tiers, a finding that must be tracked going forward, not just noted once | A single evaluation run produces segment-level tables for all three fairness dimensions without additional engineering; a segment showing more than 5 percentage points of quality difference from the overall mean is flagged explicitly in the output rather than averaged away (ties to NFR-06). |

## 5. Non-Functional Requirements

| ID | Category | Requirement | How it will be verified |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NFR-01** | Latency | Classification (intent + urgency, FR-02/FR-03) shall complete at P95 < 2 seconds per ticket. End-to-end response generation (retrieval + draft + guardrail, FR-05–FR-07) shall complete at P95 < 5 seconds per ticket for the auto-respond path. | Measured on the validation set (80 tickets) under single-request load; reported as two separate P95 figures, not conflated into one number. Traces to Project Brief §07's overall "Response latency, 95th percentile: Under 3 seconds" NFR as the live-chat-facing budget; the two-stage breakdown here reflects that classification gates routing before the heavier generation step runs, so the two budgets are not simply additive on the auto-respond path. |
| **NFR-02** | Availability | The evaluation harness and serving path shall handle API timeouts, malformed LLM JSON, and rate limits without crashing, such that a 120-ticket unattended batch run completes end-to-end even if individual calls fail. | CLAUDE.md §2, Unattended Gate Rule, verbatim: "The pipeline must handle API timeouts, malformed LLM JSON, and rate limits gracefully so a 120-ticket unattended run never crashes mid-batch." Verified by injecting simulated timeouts/malformed responses into a batch run and confirming the run completes with per-ticket error records rather than a batch-level failure. |
| **NFR-03** | Accuracy | Intent classification precision ≥ 85%; citation accuracy (cited `doc_id` actually supports the response) ≥ 95%; hallucination rate (unsupported claims) ≤ 5%. | Computed against the validation set per the targets in Project Brief §07; reported with the evaluation date and number of runs, per Project Brief §09 ("The hidden evaluation set is used once, at the end, and the date of that run is reported"). |
| **NFR-04** | Privacy | Zero occurrences of PII or `must_not_claim`-listed content in any released (non-escalated) response. | Automated scan of every released response in the validation and hidden-set runs; any single occurrence is treated as a governance failure, not a scoring loss, per the Dataset Guide's framing of `must_not_claim`. |
| **NFR-05** | Auditability | The evaluation harness shall accept `--input <path>` and `--output <path>` CLI arguments and shall never hardcode `development_tickets.json` or `validation_tickets.json`; every decision shall persist to the Decision Log DB (SQLite) with complete coverage. | CLAUDE.md §2, verbatim requirement; verified by running the harness against a renamed/relocated copy of the validation file via CLI arguments only, and confirming it produces identical output to the same file at its original path. Decision Log completeness checked by row-count equality against ticket-count processed. |
| **NFR-06** | Fairness | Quality (FCR-equivalent auto-vs-escalate correctness, citation accuracy) shall differ by no more than 5 percentage points between any two `customer_tier`, `customer_region`, or `language_fluency` segments, or the difference shall be explicitly reported and explained if it exceeds that. | Project Brief §07, Governance conditions: "Quality across customer groups: Under five percentage points of difference between groups." Computed per FR-11's segment tables on every evaluation run. Per discovery Section 6 (Contradiction 2), the current human-handled baseline already exceeds this band for enterprise vs. business tier (FCR gap of ~11 points) — the system is explicitly not required to inherit or launder that pre-existing gap, and NFR-06 is measured against the system's own outputs, not the historical baseline. |

## 6. Explicit Out-of-Scope Boundaries

| Not building | Why not | What would have to change for this to be reconsidered |
|---|---|---|
| Automated responses for tickets flagged `must_not_auto_respond` (security_incident, compliance_request, and related mandatory-escalation classes — 17.4% of development-set volume, 87 of 189 escalations per discovery Section 6, Contradiction 1) | Daniel (Interview 3) names security/account-compromise, billing disputes, and data-location issues as categories where automated commitments or guesses are unacceptable; the Project Brief treats governance conditions as pass/fail, not targets to approach | Only with an explicit, written policy change from CloudServe leadership and a separate, much stricter validation process for that specific category — not a model-confidence improvement |
| Answering `feature_request`, roadmap/timing questions, or genuinely novel incidents | Ines (Interview 4): "there is no answer to retrieve" for these by design; Stage 1 Section 2 confirms 28.6% of tickets are not `answerable_from_docs` | Only if a documented, reviewed answer source is created for these categories — this is a documentation-authoring project, not a retrieval-tuning one |
| Using agents' private, unreviewed answer files as a data or training source | Daniel (Interview 3): some private answers "were correct two years ago and have not been right since"; Ines has asked twice for these to be consolidated and it has not happened — they are explicitly unreviewed | Only after CloudServe runs its own review/consolidation of those files into the governed knowledge base; this system builds only from the 29-article reviewed corpus |
| Multi-turn conversational follow-up / clarifying-question dialogue | No stakeholder interview describes or requests this; the dataset and ground-truth schema are both single-shot (ticket in, one reference response out); building it would be inventing a requirement not present in any discovery evidence | Only if a future discovery pass surfaces an explicit need for it — flagged here, not assumed |
| Tuning the production decision threshold against the hidden 120-ticket evaluation set, or repeated runs against it | Project Brief §09: "The hidden evaluation set is used once, at the end, and the date of that run is reported"; Dataset Guide: "tuning against a set until the number looks good is self-defeating" | Never, by design — this is a governance rule of the capstone, not a product decision |

## 7. Success Metrics & Sentence Test

| Measure | Baseline | Target | Measured how | Reported by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-contact resolution (FCR) | 42% (Marcus, Interview 1) / **43.8% measured directly from the development set**, Stage 1 Section 2 | **65%** (per the user-specified target; the Project Brief's two internal tables state 60% and 65% respectively — this PRD adopts 65% as the binding target and flags the brief's internal inconsistency as an open question, Section 9) | Tickets closed without escalation ÷ total tickets, per Dataset Guide field definitions | Support Leadership (Marcus), from the evaluation harness output |
| Time to first reply | 8–12 hours (Interview 1; not independently re-derivable from the ticket schema, which has no first-response-time field — Stage 1 Section 2 note) | Under 2 hours (SLA) / under 5 minutes for auto-respond path (Project Brief §07) | Mean interval between ticket arrival and first reply, logged at response-generation time for the automated path | Support Leadership |
| Customer satisfaction | 3.2 / 5 (Project Brief §01); **2.97/5 measured directly from the development set**, Stage 1 Section 2 | 4.0–4.2 / 5 | Mean `csat_rating` across reviewed/responded tickets | Support Leadership |
| Escalation rate | 58% (Project Brief §01) / **56.2% measured**, Stage 1 Section 2 | 30% or lower, with the composition shift explicit: discretionary escalations (currently 54% of all escalations, Section 6 Contradiction 1) are the lever this system pulls — mandatory escalations (46%) are not expected to shrink and should not be targeted | Tickets routed to escalation ÷ total tickets, split by mandatory vs. discretionary (FR-04) | Support Leadership |
| Policy breaches (automated response to a `must_not_auto_respond` ticket) | Not applicable pre-system (no automation exists today) | **0%** — a governance condition, not a target to approach, per Project Brief §07 | Count of `must_not_auto_respond=True` tickets that received an automated response, in any evaluation run | Support Leadership + compliance review (Marcus's autumn review, Interview 1) |

**The sentence test (carried forward from Stage 1 Section 4, restated against this system's targets):**
- *This project will have been worth doing if, within three months of launch, we see* FCR rise from the 43.8% measured baseline toward the 65% target, with zero `must_not_auto_respond` policy breaches and no more than a 5-point fairness gap introduced by the system itself (NFR-06).
- *We will know it did not work if we see* FCR flat or falling, discretionary escalation volume unchanged, any policy breach at all, or the enterprise-tier quality gap identified in Stage 1 Section 6 (Contradiction 2) persisting or widening under the new system.
- *The measure CloudServe leadership will actually be judged on internally is* the 2-hour first-response SLA, even though Marcus names FCR as what he actually cares about — this PRD tracks both, explicitly, rather than optimizing the reported metric at the expense of the one that matters (Stage 1 Section 4).

## 8. Assumptions & Risk Log

| Assumption | Why you believe it | What happens if it is false | How you will find out |
|---|---|---|---|
| The routing confidence threshold (illustrative 0.80 in Project Brief §05) can be set from development/validation data alone and will generalize to the hidden set | The hidden set is drawn from the same population and schema (Dataset Guide) | Auto-respond rate or FCR on the hidden set diverges sharply from validation-set performance, indicating threshold overfitting | Run the harness once on the hidden set at submission and compare its auto-respond/escalate split and FCR against validation-set figures; discrepancy is reported, not corrected post hoc |
| `must_not_auto_respond=True` and the `expected_route` label fully and correctly capture every category that must never be automated | This is the labeling scheme given in the dataset and used throughout discovery (Stage 1 Section 6) | A real-world ticket type requiring mandatory escalation exists but is not captured by these flags in the dataset, and the deployed system could auto-respond to it | **RESOLVED (2026-11-11), Stage 3 audit:** full enumeration of the development set found exactly this gap for `data_residency` — only 69% of its tickets carry the flag, but Daniel Okonkwo's interview testimony names the category as a compliance risk even for humans. `data_residency` was added to `MANDATORY_ESCALATION_INTENTS` as a whole-intent policy extension beyond the raw flag (FR-04, `src/routing/policy.py`). No other gap was found by the same full-enumeration check. |
| The 29-article knowledge base is sufficient to cover the 71.4% of tickets marked `answerable_from_docs=True` | This is the dataset's own label, assumed accurate per the Dataset Guide | Retrieval returns a plausible-but-wrong passage for a ticket the label says is answerable, which the Project Brief names as worse than retrieving nothing | Citation-accuracy metric (NFR-03) on the validation set; a shortfall here is a retrieval-tuning problem, not a scope problem, and is reported as such |
| The confidence score from classification (FR-02) is a genuine probability estimate that can be calibrated, not just a model-reported number | Project Brief §04 explicitly separates "a confidence number" from "a confidence number that does correspond to the probability of being right" as the thing to get right | Stated confidence diverges from observed accuracy by more than 5 points (violating the calibration governance condition, Project Brief §07) | Calibration check on the validation set before any threshold is finalized; recalibration (e.g., temperature scaling or isotonic regression) is budgeted as contingency work if this fails |
| **Risk:** the system answers confidently and incorrectly on an answerable-looking but actually ambiguous ticket | Marcus (Interview 1): "I would rather it said nothing than said something wrong"; Ravi (Interview 5): he will act on a confident wrong answer and "break something" | A real customer receives and acts on an incorrect automated reply — the single worst outcome named across the interviews | Mitigation: FR-06/FR-07 guardrails default to escalation on any doubt; monitored via NFR-03's hallucination-rate metric and reviewed before any threshold change is accepted |
| **Risk:** enterprise-tier quality gap (Stage 1 Section 6, Contradiction 2) is reproduced or worsened by the automated system | The gap exists in the human-handled baseline today and nothing about automation guarantees it closes | Enterprise customers, the tier CloudServe is most anxious about (Marcus, Interview 1), continue to receive the worst service of any tier — now from a system rather than from understaffing | Mitigation: FR-11/NFR-06 segment reporting on every run, reviewed specifically for the enterprise segment, not just the aggregate |
